02Cloud Computing

Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of computing services, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, and analytics, over the Internet. Instead of directly purchasing, installing, and operating expensive physical hardware or on-premises data centers, individuals and organizations can flexibly rent resources from major cloud providers such as AWS, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud.

This model works on a pay-as-you-go basis, so you pay only for what you actually use. It makes it easy for systems to scale up or down as demand changes, increases availability, reduces the cost of infrastructure investment, and lets applications reach users around the world quickly.

03Cloud Services

IaaS Infrastructure as a Service
Provides virtual infrastructure such as servers, networking, and storage. Users manage the operating system and their own applications.
Example: AWS EC2
PaaS Platform as a Service
Provides a platform for developing and running applications. The provider manages the underlying infrastructure and runtime.
Example: Heroku
SaaS Software as a Service
Complete software accessed through a web browser. Users just use it, without installing or maintaining anything.
Example: Gmail
